      <h2>What belongs on a dashboard</h2>
      <p>Three things and only three: what changed vs baseline, who is currently below threshold, and what needs a decision today. Everything else is a report — save it for the weekly.</p>
      <h2>Dashboard per role</h2>
      <p><strong>Manager (30-second view):</strong> 5 KPIs with trend, red alerts, best recordings of the day. Refreshed daily.<br><strong>Supervisor:</strong> real-time queues, per-agent live status, current-shift QA flags. Refreshed live.<br><strong>Executive:</strong> weekly rollups of CSAT/FCR/QA score, cost per contact, headcount trend.</p>
      <h2>From metrics to actions</h2>
      <p>The most impactful pattern: a "requires action" section on the manager dashboard — specific agents, specific calls, with a clear next step. This is where content-based KPIs shine: an alert is only useful if it's tied to a call you can play.</p>
      <h2>Free templates</h2>
      <p>Excel and Power BI templates for exec, supervisor and agent dashboards. Structure works with any data source; drop in your ACD, survey and QA feeds.</p>
      <h2>Common mistakes</h2>
      <ul>
        <li>Too many metrics — anything over 7 on a single screen becomes wallpaper.</li>
        <li>No baseline — a number without context isn't information.</li>
        <li>Vanity over action — showing "we're doing great" instead of "here's what to fix."</li>
      </ul>
