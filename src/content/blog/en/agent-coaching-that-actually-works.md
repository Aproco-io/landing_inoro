Most contact centers run coaching quarterly. Agent stats say the ratings barely change from one quarter to the next — so coaching gets blamed. In fact, **the cadence is the problem**, not the coaches.

Below is a model that works — the **3-30-90 rhythm** — and how AI-scored calls make it possible.

## The 3-30-90 rhythm

- **3 minutes** of feedback within 24h of any high-signal call (great, terrible, first hard case)
- **30 minutes** weekly 1-1 covering patterns and one skill to improve
- **90 days** as the horizon for measurable behavior change

Each layer serves a different purpose:
- 3-minute feedback fixes muscle memory
- Weekly 1-1 builds identity as a specific kind of agent ("the one who's great at empathy")
- 90-day horizon lets change show up in the metrics

## Why it fails without AI

Classical QA can't feed the 3-minute layer. If reviewers sample 3% of calls, most agents have zero high-signal moments captured in a given week. The whole rhythm collapses to whatever the reviewer happened to catch.

With AI scoring every call:
- **Great calls are surfaced automatically** (all "excellent" scores → 3-min praise message)
- **Terrible calls surface too** (all "compliance risk" flags → same-day supervisor conversation)
- **Weekly 1-1 has real data** (not "here's what I remember")

## What supervisors actually do differently

Before: 4 hours a week listening to random calls, hoping to find something useful.

After: 4 hours a week reviewing AI-scored highlights, running 3-minute check-ins, prepping weekly 1-1s from actual patterns.

The work shifts from *finding signal* to *acting on signal*.

## Numbers you'll see

Typical operations rolling out the 3-30-90 rhythm with AI-scored calls report:

- **QA score** up 12-18% in the first 90 days
- **Agent tenure** up 20-30% year-over-year (less churn)
- **Time to competence** for new agents down from 6-8 weeks to 3-4

## What next

The rhythm is free. The AI is the multiplier that makes it feasible. Start by picking your top 3 QA criteria and running the 3-30-90 loop just on those. Once it's stable, expand.
